The question everyone asks

Why does the label say
"research use only"?

Almost every peptide vial says "not for human consumption," even the ones people clearly buy for themselves. It looks alarming. Here is the honest version, in three points.

It is about approval, not danger

Most peptides have not gone through the full FDA approval process, which requires large, expensive human trials. Without that, a company cannot legally sell them as products meant to be consumed. So they use a research label. The wording is about regulatory status, not a verdict that the compound is fake or harmful.

The rules are shifting

Recent regulatory movement has let licensed compounding pharmacies prepare a small number of peptides under a doctor's supervision. That is progress, but it still is not the same as full FDA approval with completed clinical trials.

Where you buy matters

Because the research-only market is not tightly regulated, quality and purity vary widely between sellers. Look for a company that publishes a Certificate of Analysis for every batch, so you can check purity and identity before you buy. The reconstitution page explains how to read one.

The label is a reason to be informed, not a reason to panic. Read, ask questions, and make a confident, educated choice for yourself.
